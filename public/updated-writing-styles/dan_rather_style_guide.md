# Dan Rather Writing Style Guide

## Introduction

This style guide captures the distinctive writing and speaking characteristics of Dan Rather, the legendary American broadcast journalist who served as anchor of the CBS Evening News for 24 years (1981-2005). Known for his direct questioning, colorful "Ratherisms," and commitment to on-the-ground reporting, Rather developed a unique voice that balanced journalistic integrity with personal style. This guide aims to identify and explain the key elements of his communication style to help writers emulate his voice effectively.

## Core Stylistic Elements

### 1. Folksy Metaphors and Similes ("Ratherisms")

Rather's most distinctive stylistic feature is his use of colorful, often rural-inspired metaphors and similes:

- **Comparative structures**: "This race is tight like a too-small bathing suit in a too-long ride home from the beach"
- **Weather-related**: "Hotter than a Laredo parking lot"
- **Animal references**: "If a frog had side pockets, he'd carry a handgun"
- **Tension descriptors**: "Tick-tight here" and "You can't get a cigarette paper between 'em"
- **Rural imagery**: "Cross as a snapping turtle"

These expressions should be:
- Rooted in everyday experiences, particularly rural or working-class contexts
- Unexpected and vivid enough to be memorable
- Used sparingly for maximum impact
- Delivered with complete seriousness despite their humorous nature

### 2. Direct, Assertive Questioning

Rather's approach to interviews and analysis features pointed, sometimes confrontational questioning:

- **Direct challenges**: "But Mr. President, would you address the specific allegation?"
- **Follow-up persistence**: "With respect, you haven't answered the question"
- **Contrast questions**: "You say X, but the record shows Y. How do you reconcile that?"
- **Pointed specificity**: Asking for exact details, dates, and figures

Questions should be:
- Straightforward and unambiguous
- Focused on substantive issues rather than personalities
- Persistent when initial answers are evasive
- Respectful in tone while firm in substance

### 3. Measured Delivery with Strategic Emphasis

Rather's speaking rhythm balances journalistic efficiency with dramatic emphasis:

- **Varied sentence length**: Alternating between concise statements and more elaborate constructions
- **Strategic pauses**: Using silence effectively for emphasis
- **Controlled pacing**: Speaking at a deliberate rate (approximately 120-130 words per minute)
- **Emphasis on key words**: Stressing unexpected syllables or words for attention

Delivery should be:
- Controlled and authoritative
- Varied in pace according to content importance
- Deliberate rather than rushed
- Employing pauses before and after key points

### 4. Texan-Influenced Vocabulary

Rather's Texas background influences his word choice and expressions:

- **Regional expressions**: Incorporating distinctly Southern and Texan phrases
- **Rural vocabulary**: Drawing from agricultural and outdoor experiences
- **Place-specific references**: Occasionally referencing Texas locations for comparisons
- **Working-class expressions**: Incorporating language from blue-collar settings

Vocabulary should be:
- Authentic to Rather's background without becoming caricature
- Accessible to general audiences despite regional influences
- Colorful but not distracting from the substance
- Consistent with formal journalistic contexts despite folksy elements

### 5. Emotional Engagement with Restraint

Rather allows more emotional connection to stories than some contemporaries, while maintaining journalistic standards:

- **Controlled emotion**: Maintaining composure while allowing appropriate emotional response
- **Authentic reactions**: Occasional genuine displays of emotion (e.g., during national tragedies)
- **Dignified solemnity**: Conveying appropriate gravity during serious news
- **Restrained enthusiasm**: Showing genuine interest without sensationalism

Emotional expression should be:
- Authentic rather than performative
- Proportional to the significance of events
- Controlled but not completely suppressed
- Secondary to factual reporting

### 6. Narrative Approach to News

Rather frames news as stories with human elements, not just collections of facts:

- **Narrative arcs**: Developing clear beginnings, middles, and conclusions
- **Character focus**: Highlighting key figures and their motivations
- **Scene setting**: Establishing locations and circumstances
- **Contextual framing**: Placing current events within historical patterns

Narrative elements should:
- Enhance understanding rather than dramatize
- Remain grounded in factual reporting
- Connect individual events to broader significance
- Emphasize human impact of news events


## Rhetorical Techniques

### 1. Strategic Repetition

Rather uses repetition for emphasis and clarity:

- **Key phrase reinforcement**: Repeating crucial information
- **Parallel structures**: Using similar grammatical patterns for related ideas
- **Bookending**: Beginning and ending segments with similar language
- **Incremental development**: Building on previously stated information

Repetition should be:
- Purposeful rather than accidental
- Varied enough to avoid monotony
- Focused on essential information
- Rhythmic rather than mechanical

### 2. Balanced Contrasts

Rather frequently employs contrasting elements:

- **Opposing viewpoints**: "Some say X, while others maintain Y"
- **Contradictory evidence**: "The official statement claims X, but documents show Y"
- **Expectation vs. reality**: "What was anticipated as X turned out to be Y"
- **Past vs. present**: "Previously X, but now Y"

Contrasts should:
- Highlight genuine tensions or contradictions
- Present multiple perspectives fairly
- Avoid false equivalencies
- Lead toward deeper understanding

### 3. Transitional Techniques

Rather employs several methods to move between topics:

- **Thematic bridges**: Connecting seemingly disparate stories through common themes
- **Temporal markers**: "Meanwhile," "Earlier today," "At this hour"
- **Geographic shifts**: "From Washington to Moscow," "Turning now to events in..."
- **Topical pivots**: "On another front," "In other developments"

Transitions should be:
- Clear and unambiguous
- Smooth rather than jarring
- Logical in their connections
- Varied throughout a piece

### 4. Qualification and Attribution

Rather carefully qualifies statements and attributes information:

- **Source identification**: "According to White House officials," "Pentagon sources say"
- **Certainty markers**: "What we know for certain," "What remains unclear"
- **Verification language**: "CBS News has confirmed," "Reports that have not been independently verified"
- **Perspective signals**: "From the administration's viewpoint," "Critics contend"

Qualification should:
- Maintain journalistic integrity
- Distinguish between fact, analysis, and opinion
- Acknowledge limitations of information
- Provide appropriate context for claims

## Content Approach

### 1. Fact-Centered Foundation

Rather's approach prioritizes factual reporting as the foundation:

- **Verified information**: Emphasizing confirmed facts
- **Multiple sources**: Drawing from various perspectives
- **Specific details**: Including precise figures, dates, and names
- **Chronological clarity**: Establishing clear timelines

Factual elements should:
- Form the core of all content
- Be presented before analysis or commentary
- Be specific rather than general
- Be verified rather than assumed

### 2. Historical Context

Rather frequently places current events in historical perspective:

- **Historical parallels**: Connecting present situations to past events
- **Pattern recognition**: Identifying recurring themes or developments
- **Institutional memory**: Referencing previous coverage or developments
- **Long-view perspective**: Considering how current events fit into broader historical narratives

Historical context should:
- Enhance understanding of current events
- Be relevant rather than tangential
- Provide genuine insight rather than superficial comparison
- Acknowledge both similarities and differences with past events

### 3. Power Analysis

Rather's reporting often examines power dynamics:

- **Institutional scrutiny**: Examining actions of government, corporations, and other powerful entities
- **Accountability focus**: Questioning those in authority
- **Impact assessment**: Considering how decisions affect ordinary people
- **Systemic perspective**: Looking beyond individuals to institutional patterns

Power analysis should:
- Apply scrutiny equally across political spectrum
- Focus on substantive issues rather than personalities
- Consider multiple perspectives on power relationships
- Maintain journalistic independence from power centers

### 4. Human Element

Rather incorporates human impact into reporting:

- **Individual stories**: Including experiences of people affected by events
- **Emotional dimension**: Acknowledging feelings generated by news events
- **Relatable framing**: Making complex issues understandable through human examples
- **Universal concerns**: Focusing on broadly relatable aspects of stories

Human elements should:
- Illustrate rather than replace factual reporting
- Avoid exploitation or sensationalism
- Represent diverse perspectives and experiences
- Connect individual stories to broader patterns

## Practical Application

To effectively emulate Dan Rather's writing style:

1. **Begin with solid factual reporting** as the foundation
2. **Incorporate occasional colorful metaphors** ("Ratherisms") for emphasis
3. **Use direct, straightforward language** for most content
4. **Vary sentence length and structure** for rhythm and emphasis
5. **Include relevant historical context** to deepen understanding
6. **Ask direct, substantive questions** when presenting different viewpoints
7. **Acknowledge emotional dimensions** while maintaining journalistic distance
8. **Use Texan or rural expressions sparingly** for color rather than as a constant feature
9. **Maintain an authoritative tone** without becoming rigid or impersonal
10. **Conclude with context or significance** rather than merely ending

## Examples of Rather's Style

> "This race is tight like a too-small bathing suit in a too-long ride home from the beach."

This classic Ratherism demonstrates his use of unexpected, vivid metaphors to describe political situations in relatable terms.

> "From Dallas, Texas, the flash apparently official: President Kennedy died at 1 p.m. Central Standard Time, 2:00 Eastern Standard Time, some 38 minutes ago."

This somber report shows Rather's direct, factual approach during breaking news, with precise details and controlled emotion despite the gravity of the situation.

> "If a frog had side pockets, he'd carry a handgun."

This Ratherism illustrates his use of rural-inspired absurdist metaphors to make a point about the pointlessness of hypothetical scenarios.

> "To say that we are closer to victory today is to believe, in the face of the evidence, the optimists who have been wrong in the past. To suggest we are on the edge of defeat is to yield to unreasonable pessimism. To say that we are mired in stalemate seems the only realistic, yet unsatisfactory, conclusion."

This excerpt from Rather's Vietnam War commentary demonstrates his use of parallel structure, balanced presentation of perspectives, and measured conclusion based on evidence.

## Conclusion

Dan Rather's communication style combines journalistic rigor with personal distinctiveness. His approach balances factual reporting with colorful expression, authoritative presence with emotional engagement, and direct questioning with narrative storytelling. By incorporating his distinctive "Ratherisms," direct questioning style, measured delivery, and commitment to factual reporting with context, writers can effectively capture the essence of Rather's unique voice while maintaining journalistic integrity.
